speakwrite

Prove a human wrote it.

Not by analyzing the output — by witnessing the process.

Speakwrite is an open protocol that proves a human wrote a document.

The Problem

AI can now produce text indistinguishable from human writing. Every detection tool that analyzes the output is losing an arms race it can never win. The next model will always evade the last detector.

The Approach

Speakwrite takes a fundamentally different approach: instead of analyzing what was written, it instruments how it was written. The protocol captures keystroke dynamics — timing, rhythm, pauses, corrections — during composition, and cryptographically binds that behavioral evidence to the resulting document.

The result is a proof bundle: a portable, verifiable artifact that anyone can independently check. No central authority. No API keys. No trust required.

How It Works

  1. Write Use the Speakwrite editor or any compatible observer. Your keystroke dynamics are captured locally — nothing leaves your device until you publish.
  2. Prove When you're ready, Speakwrite generates a cryptographic commitment chain binding your behavioral evidence to your document. This is your proof bundle.
  3. Publish Post your proof to the AT Protocol (Bluesky, self-hosted PDS, or any compatible network). Or export the JSON bundle and share it however you want.
  4. Verify Anyone with the document and the proof bundle can verify it in their browser. The verifier is open source and runs entirely client-side.

What It Proves (and What It Doesn't)

It proves

A document was produced through a physical typing process exhibiting characteristics consistent with human motor behavior.

It doesn't prove

That the ideas are original, that no AI was consulted, or that the author didn't transcribe AI output. It raises the cost of deception by orders of magnitude — it doesn't make it impossible.

This honesty is a design choice, not a limitation. Speakwrite produces forensic evidence, not mathematical certainty.

Key Properties

Open protocol
The proof bundle JSON format is the protocol. Anyone can build an observer, a verifier, or a publishing tool.
Privacy-preserving
Raw keystrokes never leave your device. Only aggregate statistical features are committed.
Decentralized
No central server, no trusted authority. Verification is pure client-side cryptography (SHA-256).
Progressive trust
A single proof is weak evidence. A corpus of proofs from the same identity, exhibiting consistent behavioral patterns over time, is strong evidence.
AT Protocol native
Publish proofs to any AT Protocol PDS. Resolve and verify from any federated node.

For Whom

  • Journalists establishing that reporting was composed by a human author
  • Students providing evidence of their own writing process
  • Writers who want provenance for works where human authorship matters
  • Publishers building trust signals into their content
  • Anyone who wants to say "I wrote this" and have it mean something

Open Source

Speakwrite is open source under CC BY-SA 4.0. The protocol spec, editor, verifier, and core library are all public.

  • Protocol specification
  • Core library: @speakwrite/core (TypeScript, Web Crypto API)
  • Editor: PWA with offline support
  • Verifier: Standalone client-side verification page
  • AT Protocol integration: Custom lexicon io.speakwrite.proof

Status

Working draft. The protocol spec, web editor, and verifier are functional. Self-hosted PDS support is built in. The proof bundle format is stable enough to build on.

Speakwrite does not solve the AI problem. It creates a new kind of evidence that didn't exist before.
